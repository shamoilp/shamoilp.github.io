What the checker leaves out

  • You choose the payment type. It does not read your contract, and what the work actually is decides the category.
  • It has no treaty rule for rent, freight, insurance or software payments ZATCA treats as other income. A treaty may still give relief on those.
  • Its treaty rules start on 1 Jan 2025. For earlier payments it shows the domestic rate and sends you to an adviser.
  • It has no rates for payments before 19 Mar 2014.
  • Software and databases follow ZATCA's January 2024 guideline, which covers domestic law only. Under a treaty ZATCA assesses them case by case, so any treaty figure is conditional.
  • ZATCA says the scope of royalties is still under discussion, so other licences and know-how go to an adviser when they could be read another way.
  • Directors' fees, how the delay fine treats part of a 30-day period, and how it is counted when the 10th falls on a weekend or public holiday, follow a practitioner's position because ZATCA has published no rule on them. Answers say so and show the other readings.

How the rates were checked

  • Domestic rates come from Income Tax Law art. 68 and Bylaws art. 63, read in a consolidated English translation up to October 2020. The Arabic text prevails.
  • Software categories and the royalty definition come from ZATCA's software guideline and its General Guideline for Withholding Tax, May 2026, read in English. The Arabic text prevails.
  • Rates for 58 treaties come from a secondary compilation. Rates for 5 were read from the treaty text ZATCA publishes.
  • A qualified practitioner reviewed and signed off every rule and treaty record. This is not a legal opinion.
